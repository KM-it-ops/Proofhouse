A small oven, a long memory

Maple & Rye began when baker Elena Marchetti traded a restaurant kitchen for a brick oven behind a former bookshop on Hawthorn Lane. The name comes from the two grains she refuses to rush: rye for depth, maple for the sugar we boil down from trees along the creek.

Every loaf here proofed in cloth, not plastic. Our pastry lead, Jonah Weiss, sources butter from a dairy forty minutes north and folds it until the dough shatters like frost. When you smell cardamom in the alley, it's because we're testing a new Saturday special—not because a diffuser said so.

We are not the biggest bakery in the city. We are the one where the barista next door saves you a heel of sourdough when you forget your wallet. That is the neighbourhood we bake for.
